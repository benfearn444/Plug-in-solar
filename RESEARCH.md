# Bright Socket — launch research notes

## Positioning

This prototype is intentionally a pre-launch catalogue. It does not claim that any product is legally approved. A listing should only move to checkout after the complete kit has been checked against the current UK framework and the supplier has provided supporting evidence.

The current legislative milestone is the Plugs and Sockets etc. (Safety) Regulations 1994 and Electricity Safety, Quality and Continuity Regulations 2002 (Amendment) Regulations 2026 (SI 2026/848), listed as coming into force on 27 August 2026. The commencement of the framework does not by itself prove that a particular kit is compliant.

## Current screening gates

- Final GB plug-in solar specification: maximum apparent power of 800 VA and maximum current of 3.5 A.
- The final specification permits one device per household final ring circuit, while current G98 network requirements restrict this to one device per household unless amended.
- The AC connection should use a manufacturer-supplied BS 1363 plug; the specification includes half-insulated pins and a BS 1362 fuse not exceeding 5A.
- Total PV module input may not exceed 2000W; products above 960W should advise consumers to consider professional assessment of the existing electrical installation. This makes the 1260W candidate a higher-scrutiny product even though its inverter export is 799 VA.
- Outdoor plugs and socket-outlets need at least IP55 protection; multiway adaptors, RCD adaptors and unsuitable connection arrangements must not be used.
- Mounting systems must be supplied as part of the complete kit where required; cable ties, rope, string, adhesive tape and bungee cords alone are not permitted as mounting arrangements.
- The framework excludes plug-in batteries and solar products integrated with batteries.
- Manufacturers must type-test devices to G98 requirements and register compliant devices on the ENA Type Test Register before placing them on the market.
- PV modules, cables, connectors, inverter behaviour, EMC, IP rating and mounting stability need to be checked as part of the complete product.
- Supplier documentation should include test reports, calculations and conformity evidence; the specification states technical records are retained for at least 10 years after last manufacture.

## Supplier leads to contact

1. UKSOL — strongest first outreach target: it publicly provides 515W, 890W and 1260W kit datasheets and has a reseller enquiry route. Its published 890W and 1260W datasheets state 799 VA and 3.5 A, but the public documents do not yet prove the complete kit’s IPS conformity or the required plug/fuse details. Request wholesale pricing, stock dates, full IPS evidence, warranties and fulfilment terms.
2. iHelios — an official UKSOL partner/reseller with public pre-order pages for the 515W, 890W and 1260W range. Request reseller terms, the same manufacturer evidence pack, shipping and returns arrangements. Treat “battery ready” language as a point requiring clarification because battery-integrated products are outside the IPS scope.
3. UK Solar Kits — TSUN ESK400 Universal kit listed at £499.95. The page says it includes a UK plug cable and several mounting options, but more evidence is needed on the exact plug, fuse, contact protection, testing and mounting calculations.

An open-web search on 15 August 2026 found no indexed ENA Type Test Register result for the model references currently recorded. Because the register may not be fully indexed by ordinary search, this is a supplier question rather than a definitive absence finding: request the exact ENA record for each SKU.

## Before taking payment

Confirm the commencement date and final wording of the regulations, obtain supplier evidence, establish the seller/importer responsibility, obtain product liability cover, agree warranty/returns handling, and have the customer-facing compliance and safety copy reviewed by a suitably qualified UK professional.
